# Is the covering bound for the least Goldbach summand already known? A literature check

Status: the consultant's note of 25 Nov 2026, about 12:50 UTC (server clock), written at the research session's request for the lead.
Method: a search agent (arXiv API keyword searches over all years and over 2026, about 25 web searches, direct reads of OEIS, CWI reports, erdosproblems.com pages and arXiv papers; about 140 tool calls) reported; I then read the two key papers myself from their PDFs.
Marks: **[V]** = I read it in the source myself (PDFs of Shi v6 pages 1–2 and 13–16, Radomskii v3 pages 1–3); **[V2]** = read verbatim in the arXiv HTML text by a second fetch agent, which I asked to confirm the statements (Theorem 6.22 of Shi; Theorems 1.1 to 1.3 and Lemma 3.1 of Radomskii); **[A]** = the first search agent's report only (it says which of these it read in full); **[I]** = inference, by the agent or by me, not stated by the authors. Nothing here is a referee's judgement of any 2026 paper.

Notation: g(N) = the least prime p with N − p prime (called p(n) in the literature); ln_k = the k-fold logarithm.
Our results: **(a)** for every A there are infinitely many even N with g(N) ≥ A·ln N·ln ln N (kernel-checked in Lean, commit 1f1b090); **(b)** g(N) ≥ c·ln N·(ln₂N)²·ln₄N/(ln₃N)² infinitely often in the desert sense (paper level, `COHERENT_BLOCK.md`; now unconditionally kernel-checked on `[X/2, X]` in `goldbach/lean/GoldbachGaps/RankinSmooth.lean` as `eventually_exists_even_le_rankin` and `exists_pos_eventually_exists_even_le_rankin` via `exists_rankin_smooth_bound`).

Follow-up formalization **(c)** in `goldbach/lean/GoldbachGaps/IteratedLogBound.lean` (28 Nov 2026): conditional on the explicit hypothesis parameters `SimultaneousRootsStatement`, matching `eventually_simultaneous_roots` in `openai/LongGapsBetweenPrimes` at lines 3480 to 3485, and `MontgomeryVaughanStatement`, matching Montgomery–Vaughan 1975 Theorem 1 (`E(X) ≤ C·X^(1−δ)`), the theorems `eventually_exists_goldbach_iterated_log` and `eventually_exists_isLeastSummand_iterated_log` prove in Lean's kernel that for all large X some even N ∈ [X/2, X] possesses a Goldbach representation with g(N) ≥ c·ln X·(ln₂X)³·ln₄X/(ln₃X)².

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Before 2026 no omega-result for g(N) was found**, only heuristics, conjectures and computations [A]. The search covered arXiv by keyword, OEIS, the CWI reports of Granville–van de Lune–te Riele and Deshouillers–te Riele–Saouter, the Oliveira e Silva project page, erdosproblems.com, and the prime-gap and Jacobsthal literature as quoted in its modern introductions. Coverage of pre-2026 sources is incomplete: Westzynthius, Erdős 1935, Rankin 1938, Pomerance's 1989 chapter and Prachar's book were not read (section 5).
2. **Shi, arXiv:2605.21529 (v1 19 May, v5 13 Sep, v6 22 Sep 2026): max over even n ≤ X of p(n) ≥ (1/2 + o(1))·ln X·ln ln X, by pigeonhole and the prime number theorem alone, and p(n) > ψ(n) ln n ln ln n for almost all n [V: abstract and open-problem list; Theorem 6.22 verbatim by the second agent [V2]].** Weaker than (a) and (b). The author says the order of the maximum is open up to (ln X)²·ln ln X, and that improving the constant needs a covering construction.
3. **Radomskii, arXiv:2610.14127 (v1 9 Oct, v3 1 Nov 2026), Theorem 1.1 [V]: for every set B ⊆ ℤ≥0 and X ≥ X*, with H = ⌊c*·ln X·ln ln X/(B(X) + 1)⌋ ≥ 1, there is a block of H consecutive integers in [X/2, X] disjoint from ℙ + B.** It is not stated there as a Goldbach result [V for pages 1–3; the rest of the paper was not searched by me; the agent found no mention]. **[I] Taking B = the primes up to y gives, for large X, an even M in [X/2, X] with g(M) > y whenever π(y) + 1 ≤ (c*/2)·ln X·ln ln X, that is g(M) ≥ c′·ln X·(ln ln X)²**: (a) for every A, and more than (b) by the factor (ln₃N)²/ln₄N. I checked the logic of this specialisation (M ∉ ℙ + B means M − b is not prime for every prime b ≤ y; a block of two consecutive integers contains an even one; B(X) = π(y)). It stands or falls with the large-shift lemma of the paper (Lemma 3.1, section 3, about 24 pages), whose statement is confirmed [V2] but whose proof I have not checked; the paper is single-author and unrefereed, and says it adapts "the weight construction of [5]" (the agent identifies [5] with the OpenAI preprint below) to large shifts.
4. **So (a) and (b) are probably not new as statements if Radomskii's lemma holds.** What is independent: the proofs of (a) and of the weaker order are machine-checked in the Lean kernel and use only Chebyshev-type input, Mertens, Rankin's smooth-number bound and an average over a common integer, no weight construction.
5. **Observation, mine, unchecked ([I]; details in section 4):** if Radomskii's lemma holds as quoted, the structured block of this project should add a factor ln₂N·ln₄N/(ln₃N)² to his implied bound, giving g(N) ≥ c·ln N·(ln₂N)³·ln₄N/(ln₃N)² infinitely often. That would be new relative to everything found.
6. **Conjectured truth and data:** p(n) ≪ ln² n · ln ln n (Granville–van de Lune–te Riele 1989; Granville's sharper constants 1.51478 and 1.70098 second-hand [A]). The largest g(N) known over all even N ≤ 4·10¹⁸ is 9,781 at N = 3,325,581,707,333,960,528 (Oliveira e Silva, Herzog, Pardi) [A]. The project's record is g ≥ 160,357 at 200 digits, built by a cover rather than found by scanning.
7. **No unconditional or GRH upper bound for binary g(N)** was found [A]; the nearest is a ternary small-prime result (Harm, arXiv:2605.19566: GRH gives U = log^(4+ε) N).

## 4. Observation, not checked: the block together with the large-shift lemma

Radomskii's proof of Theorem 1.1 picks residues modulo Q = ∏_{p≤x} p by averaging, which leaves O(H·B(X)/ln x) uncovered shifts, and then covers up to ηx uncovered shifts by choosing t. For the Goldbach specialisation the shifts are −q, q prime below the bound Q*, and the number of uncovered shifts after the Chinese remainder stage must be at most ηx. **Because the lemma holds (if it holds) for every b and every set of at most ηx distinct shifts of size up to e^(3x) [V2], the Chinese remainder stage may be any cover, in particular the one of `COHERENT_BLOCK.md`** (a block of primes in (z, x/4] on a common integer N₁, greedy classes on the primes up to z): it leaves at most C·Q*·ln₂x/(ln²x·ln z) rows, instead of the κ·π(Q*)/ln x of random classes. Requiring that to be at most ηx gives Q* ≈ η′·x·ln³x·ln₃x/(ln₂x)², and with N = b + Qt ≤ e^(2x(1+o(1))), x ≈ (1/2)·ln N, that is

  g(N) ≥ c·ln N·(ln₂N)³·ln₄N/(ln₃N)²  for infinitely many even N,

against ln N·(ln₂N)² for the specialisation of Radomskii's theorem: a gain of ln₂N·ln₄N/(ln₃N)² (the survivors of the block are a factor ln x·ln₃x/(ln₂x)² fewer than those of random classes, `COHERENT_BLOCK.md` 3.1 and the Lean session's table).
Conditions and risks: the lemma's hypotheses are as in [V2] (b arbitrary, shifts distinct and at most e^(3x) in size, at most ηx of them); in our application N − q ≥ Q − Q* is larger than every prime of the range (x⁶, exp(x/(ln x)⁵)], so the divisor found makes N − q composite, as Remark 1 requires; the lemma is unrefereed and its proof has not been checked; my derivation is one paragraph and has not been written out; the rows are shifts s = −q with q up to x·ln³x, far inside the allowed |s| ≤ e^(3x), while OpenAI's Proposition 1.2 as quoted requires H ≤ x·ln²x and would not apply directly. If this holds, the Lean session could state it with the lemma as a hypothesis (status ‘kernel under a hypothesis’), reusing the three layers of `CoverEveryConstant.lean` with the mop-up layer replaced by the lemma.

### 4.1 Update (28 Nov 2026): Direct simultaneous-root and Montgomery–Vaughan bridge in Lean

Direct inspection of `openai/LongGapsBetweenPrimes` (commit `03a1190d`, `LongGapsBetweenPrimes.lean` lines 3480–3485) resolved the `H ≤ x·ln²x` limitation noted above for Proposition 1.2: the underlying lemma `eventually_simultaneous_roots` requires pairwise distinctness of the residue roots modulo each auxiliary prime `p ∈ (⌊x⁶⌋, ⌊exp(x/(ln x)⁵)⌋]` together with `k + 1 ≤ sieveDelta · x`. Because our surviving prime shifts `q < Q* ≍ η · y · (ln y)³ · ln₃ y / (ln₂ y)²` satisfy `Q* ≤ y⁶ < p` for every auxiliary prime `p`, the subtracted shifts are pairwise distinct modulo every `p`, bypassing Radomskii's Lemma 3.1 entirely.

In `goldbach/lean/GoldbachGaps/IteratedLogBound.lean`, conditional on the explicit hypothesis parameters `SimultaneousRootsStatement` and `MontgomeryVaughanStatement`, the bridge theorems `iterated_log_linear_roots_sub`, `iterated_log_translate_kill_survivors`, `iterated_log_presieve_small_survivors`, and `iterated_log_goldbach_of_disjoint_blocks` are proved in Lean's kernel following the `Prop`-parameter convention of `Effective.lean` and relying solely on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Conditional on those same explicit hypothesis parameters `SimultaneousRootsStatement` and `MontgomeryVaughanStatement`, `eventually_exists_goldbach_iterated_log` and `eventually_exists_isLeastSummand_iterated_log` combine CRT replication across disjoint subintervals of `[X/2, X]` with pigeonhole comparison against `goldbachExceptions X` to establish `g(N) ≥ c·ln X·(ln₂X)³·ln₄X/(ln₃X)²` for an even `N ∈ [X/2, X]` possessing a Goldbach representation.
