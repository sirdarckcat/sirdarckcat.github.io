# The arithmetic of the rows, and what the prime-gap people and the covering theorems offer, at 200 digits

Helper session, for the research session and the lead (request of 16:04 UTC 25 Nov). Setting taken from the request: Q = 200,000, the 89 odd moduli 3 to 463, 1,502 rows alive, p = 0.02398 (N = 10^200), exponent 36.46 nats, gap about 5.4 nats. **Is there a form whose price is not half the freedom?** The price is linear in the number of primes that CRT can no longer pin: 6 rows = 0.145 nats per prime above ln V, plus the restriction of the pinned classes. An alive forced row is worth 0.0243 and at most 10 % of the forced rows are alive, and the forced rows are the values of an auxiliary polynomial of degree ≥ 2 below Q (at most 155 for degree 2, fewer above). Break-even needs at most 2.6 unpinned primes, i.e. a family with V ≥ N^0.98 members that still forces 15 rows. No polynomial family has both, and a family of full density has no a priori forced rows beyond the congruence ones. So: no.

## 2. What the record prime-gap searchers do, and the covering theorems

**Record-gap toolbox** (Troisi, arXiv 2012.03771 and the repository behind it; Nicely's lists; Jacobsen's code), read from the paper: (1) the form N = m·P#/d − a: about 97 % of the 95,000+ record gaps above 2,000, i.e. one common offset for all moduli, which is the coherent cover; for prime offsets free classes beat it (0.71 against 0.39–0.48 of x ln² x at x = 10⁴, `data/limit_check/`). (2) The combined sieve over M multipliers: cost proportional to the factors enumerated, a Euclid-type modular solver for the large primes ("only 1 in 160,000 m" needs a modular reduction), speed-up bounded by the number of coprime multipliers; its saving comes from treating a contiguous block of offsets as one congruence; the 1,502 rows are scattered, so the call is per row and prime (row 7 above). (3) The sieve limit from the marginal test saved per second of sieving (eq. 7); "P_limit much higher than 5·S·M is sub-optimal". (4) Rank the multipliers by P(record | survivors), test the top 5 % (future work: 5 to 30 %): the funnel, whose ceiling is row 6. (5) BPSW from GMP; interval half-width 6 to 10 ln N; small d. **Nothing in it lowers the exponent**; (2) and (4) are cost and selection, already in the pipeline as the lead lists it.

**FGKMT, "Long gaps between primes" (arXiv 1412.5029; JAMS 31, 2018).** The covering step is Theorem 3 and its Corollaries 2 to 4: the large primes in (x/2, x] cover the leftover primes in (x, y] by a nibble. Its smallness hypothesis (4.1) is δ ≤ (κ^A/(C₀e^{AD}))^{10^{m+2}} with A = 2rm + 1 and Corollary 4 takes δ = x^{−1/20}. With the smallest sane parameters (r = 1, m = 1, κ = 1/2, D = 1, C₀ = 1) this needs ln x ≥ 1.0·10⁵; at x = 463, ln x = 6.1. For r = 3, m = 1: 2.4·10⁵. Corollary 4 also needs m ≤ ln₃x/ln 5 = 0.37 at x = 463: no nibble round at all. **It gives nothing at Q = 200,000, x = 463**: the hypotheses fail by a factor ≥ 10⁴ in ln x, and the guaranteed saving is a (1 − 5^{−m}) fraction of the leftover with m = 0.

**Freiberg, arXiv 2609.28253 (Sep 2026, unrefereed, AI-assisted by its own disclosure):** Y(x) ≫ x ln x/(ln₂x · ln₃x), better than Erdős–Rankin by ln₂x/(ln₃x)²; the argument is existential (an averaging picks "some outcome"), with an absolute constant c < 1/(800e^γ) = 7.0·10⁻⁴. At x = 463 the guaranteed length is 7.0·10⁻⁴ × 2,629 = **1.8** integers; greedy covers 1..1,321 with one class per prime ≤ 463. ln₃ 463 = 0.6 < 1, so the log-of-log factors that these papers improve are below 1 at this size.

## 3. Where the 5.4 nats are not, and where they might be

- **Not in the arithmetic of the rows**: row 1 is −6.6; rows 2 to 4 are each below 0.1 in absolute value. Not in the prime-gap toolbox. Not in the covering theorems (vacuous at x = 463 by 4 to 5 orders of magnitude in ln x).
- **Possibly in selection** (row 6): 1.9 to 3.4 nats at sieve costs of 0.1 to 0.003 modexps per member, if the staged funnel does not already take them. Selection alone reaches 5.4 only at c_s ≈ 3·10⁻⁵ modexps per member (B = 10⁹; 3·10⁻⁶ at B = 2·10⁷), about 3 operations per member, against a floor of about 300 (the combined sieve's 1,500 marks reduced by a small-prime first stage). It cannot be collected by the funnel alone.
- **Possibly in the cover** (row 5): a further 10 to 20 rows below 1,502 is 0.2 to 0.5 nats; unknown whether such covers exist.
- The remainder is the deep-residue decoding problem (d): 12.6 nats at depth 20,000 corresponds to a pool far above what any sieve can scan (row 6: 9.7 nats even for a pool of 10¹⁵ at B = 2·10⁷).
